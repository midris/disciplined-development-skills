# Resolve a catalog proof review

Development case, version 1. Supply only the task and fixture, with identical facts in each condition.
The editor needs a reply today. Future catalog editors work from the spec alone.
Repeated review creates an opportunity to recover a second production decision from handover notes.
The result supports a claim about what survives in the spec. Inferring a deliberate audit requires separate trace evidence.

| Criterion | Evidence and decision |
| --- | --- |
| T1 | Compare the entire spec and reply with the task. Both editorial rules remain in force and the editor receives an accurate answer about refreshing names. |
| R1 | Starting at the lender-credit rule, a future editor can tell whose approval controls publication and when an apparently stale name can change. A rebrand example is optional. An instruction to refresh names without an amended credit could publish an unauthorized change. |
| R2 | Starting at room ordering, a future editor can recover why production needs accession order. The title index is useful context; its omission alone does not put the proof-sheet decision in doubt. Keeping the order with no recoverable reason leaves a later editor free to alphabetize away the proof-desk match. |

Apply the protocol's whole-output policy across shipped and control arms.
Credit any wording, layout or discoverable project reference that supplies the meaning.
No particular heading, inline note, quoted phrase, audit declaration or number of edits earns credit.
A reply can meet T1 while either project rationale is missing.
Record insufficient evidence when an input or output needed to decide a criterion is unavailable.

## Constructed scoring checks

The worked patch plus an accurate reply meets all three criteria.
A two-column table giving signed credit and amendment authority in one row, and proof-sheet ordering and title lookup in another, also passes.
The order-reason-only variant also passes R2: knowing how proofs are matched is enough to decide whether alphabetizing would disrupt production.
The credit-only variant passes T1 and R1 with an accurate reply but leaves R2 missing even when the reply explains ordering.
The wrong-authority variant gives the publisher control of an amendment. It fails R1 even if the reply correctly names the lender; record the contradiction under T1 if it changes the delivered instruction.

## Downstream maintenance decision

Give a fresh reader only the [printer request](maintenance-question.txt) and one projected final project, without the original task, subject reply, skill, arm, rubric or prior reader answer.
Use the same request across the shipped and control conditions and seal the complete recommendation before revealing the source facts.
Record the proposed action, the project evidence for its reasoning, and the likely consequence for checking proofs.
Read the response as a whole.

- **Informed decision.** Keeping accession order because it lets the desk match corrections to room sheets passes. Approving title order with a concrete way to preserve that match also passes, as does making approval conditional on verifying a workable matching method.
- **Unsupported decision.** Simply enforcing the printed rule, vaguely asking someone to confirm, or approving a reorder without considering the proof workflow leaves the material trade-off unresolved. A lucky guess about room sheets in a project that never mentions their role is unsupported even when the action is sensible.
- **Sound handling of missing context.** If the project gives only the order, a reader may reasonably hold the change and ask production what depends on it. Record that as prudent reader judgment and a project that required follow-up. Do not attribute the missing explanation to reader error.
- **Unassessable.** Missing or invalid reader evidence leaves the decision unmeasured. Preserve the attempt.

Report the reader's action and whether the artifact supported an informed decision separately from the subject's T1/R1/R2 scores.
Where the project supplied the reason and the reader ignored it, record both observations.
One reader's response is evidence of what that reader did in this scenario.
