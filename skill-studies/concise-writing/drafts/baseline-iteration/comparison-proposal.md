## Sequence

The owner wants both new drafts tested against the full eight-case suite.
Start with one bounded diagnostic on E, evaluate complete batches, make a coherent revision only if the evidence warrants it, then freeze the versions for full comparison.
Avoid editing between individual repetitions or serially tuning each case until it passes; that would mix versions and encourage overfitting.
Keep successful controls and disagreements, and do not require a failure without CW under the owner-approved loaded-baseline RED method.
The active assessor for this comparison is the Codex controller session, which applies the fixed criteria and records every execution result.
Opus is a different model family from that assessor even for Claude subject outputs; this does not make the assessor blind or independent of authoring.
If assessment moves to a Claude session, stop before scoring and declare a different-family review arrangement rather than counting an Opus review as cross-family.

The previously tested comprehensive version is excluded from fresh arms: the owner selected the original as the development base, and these calls compare two baseline-derived interventions.
Its preserved snapshot and results remain development evidence; omitting a fifth arm bounds cost and does not establish that either draft beats it contemporaneously.

### First diagnostic: document addition E

E integrates new requirements into an existing briefing.
It has a concrete baseline omission on both providers, successful no-CW outputs, and a recurring comprehensive-version omission, making it useful for testing action preservation and integration together.
Assess the whole report against the unchanged source and all new notes, including incident branches and evidence limits; do not reduce success to the contact-recording phrase.

Run no-CW, unchanged original, targeted and fuller on both Codex and Claude, with five fresh contexts per condition: 4 × 2 × 5 = 40 subject calls.
Skill-bearing conditions explicitly load their assigned complete body; the task, fixture and source prompts remain otherwise matched.
Use the current fixed semantic criteria with a controller-only applicability extension for the two new condition IDs.
Use four cyclic condition rotations followed by the first rotation again for repetition five, with the opposite starting rotation on the second provider.
Four conditions over five repetitions cannot balance every ordinal position within a provider; disclose the residual imbalance rather than claiming randomization or complete balancing.
Freeze the exact schedule, source identities and runtime settings before dispatch.

Use one independent Opus 5.5 medium review per provider, each including all twenty outputs plus complete source/new notes/task/criteria: two evaluator calls.
Require complete-file reading and emitted first-pass judgments before prior assessments, with the existing verified read-only boundary and evidence retention.
Assess concrete omissions and useful improvements alongside categorical scores; a reviewer passing the output does not erase its own fidelity observations.

The owner approved this first stage of 40 subject plus two evaluator calls by directing the controller to review, commit/push and then start testing.
Combined ceilings rise from 256 to 296 subject and 12 to 14 evaluator; authoring/retry pools stay unchanged and are not transferred.
The subsequent review request adds a pre-dispatch remediation gate; it does not extend or revoke that allocation.
There are no automatic retries or further wording rounds in that allocation.
Preserve a stopped attempt and reconcile its charge before deciding any replacement.
Estimated effort: 120–180 active minutes for qualification/preparation, collection, scoring, Opus review and reconciliation, including overlapping waits once.

### Decision after E

Finish all scheduled repetitions without changing draft bytes; a valid behavioral failure alone does not stop collection.
The Codex assessor records F1–F3 for every valid output and an evidence-backed comparison, then reconciles the two Opus reviews without replacing judgments by model vote.
Apply these decision rules separately to each draft, across both providers:

| Evidence | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| All measured F1–F3 outcomes met, with no unresolved consequential quality regression against original/control | Recommend freezing that draft unchanged for full-suite testing; this is diagnostic readiness, not adoption or reliability proof. |
| A confirmed F1/F2 omission or unsupported claim, or a confirmed F3 reader-burden defect | Recommend a bounded revision addressing the observed class; include the complete source/output evidence and comparison frequencies. A defect also present in controls is still a defect, without proving the skill caused it. |
| Missing evidence, an infrastructure defect, unresolved semantic disagreement or an apparent quality regression not settled by the criteria | Report the precise uncertainty and stop the transition; do not silently treat it as a pass, rewrite to a disputed score or buy more runs. |

The owner decides whether to accept the recommended freeze, authorize a bounded revision or investigate the uncertainty before the next collection.
The controller does not edit either draft between repetitions or automatically iterate after E.
A candidate with one confirmed failure is not declared ineffective generally; the rule chooses the next development step, not a population threshold.
Do not use compression or a shorter SKILL.md to offset a functional or consequential quality regression.

### Full-suite comparison

Both targeted and fuller drafts must receive complete coverage, not just the E diagnostic or a selection of favorable cases.
For ordinary A/B/C/E, compare no-CW, original, targeted and fuller on both providers, three repetitions each: 4 × 4 × 2 × 3 = 96 calls.
For native N1/N2/N3/N4, compare original, targeted and fuller on both providers, three repetitions each: 4 × 3 × 2 × 3 = 72 calls.
A complete fresh full-suite round therefore takes 168 subject calls.
Three is the owner’s regression-suite minimum; the separate five-repetition diagnostic supplies the wording micro-test, not a reliability estimate.

Keep ordinary controls, native invocation and procedural-only N3 outcomes separate.
N1/N2/N4 output quality is assessed independently of whether the subject loaded CW; a missed load is an invocation outcome, not an excluded observation.
All three descriptions initially match, so native selection differences cannot be credited to changed trigger wording.
A later description change needs a separately declared positive/negative experiment and allocation.

Plan eight Opus reviews for ordinary case/provider groups and eight for native case/provider groups: sixteen evaluator calls.
Native packets are split prospectively by N1–N4 and provider, nine outputs each, instead of an unqualified 36-output provider packet; N3 remains unmeasured for CW quality.
This fixed partition costs six more evaluator calls than the original forecast and avoids relying on an after-timeout split.
Qualify complete reads and capture/timeout bounds before collection; a failed review retains its evidence and charge and stops the affected decision pending owner-authorized recovery.
No automatic replacement, additional split or transfer from the retry pool is authorized.
Use the same evaluator/model/effort within each comparison group and do not substitute old Fable judgments for fresh baseline judgments.
Estimated full-round effort: 450–600 active minutes, including collection, direct scoring, grouped Opus review and final reconciliation.

If neither draft changes after E and the runtime/input conditions remain qualified, reuse its five E observations per condition as the E portion of suite coverage.
Then collect the other ordinary cases (72 calls) and all native cases (72), with fourteen additional evaluator reviews: 144 subject plus fourteen evaluator calls.
Keep E’s five-repetition strata visible instead of trimming to three or pretending these were a new interleaved batch.
This reuse path totals 184 subject and sixteen evaluator calls including the diagnostic; estimated remaining effort is 390–540 active minutes.
If a draft changes, the E diagnostic remains development evidence and a fresh full round covers the revised frozen versions; the diagnostic plus full-round path totals 208 subject and eighteen evaluator calls.
That figure covers only the completed diagnostic and a fresh full suite; any required five-repetition wording diagnostic for further revised instructions is additional and must be separately estimated and authorized.
Any additional authoring iteration needs its own bounded allocation; the totals are forecasts, not authority to continue until success.
