The sessions agree on the textual defect in exactly eight outputs: Codex original repetitions 1/2/3/5, and Claude original 1/4, targeted 4 and fuller 3.
Those outputs require confirming that a team has a named fallback contact, but no longer require recording it before invitation.
The Codex-output reviewer fails F1/F2 for that loss; the Claude-output reviewer marks it as a weakness because the contact check and manager's decision survive.
The latter explicitly acknowledges that the strict reading reproduces the active counts and is at least as defensible; its initial all-pass judgment remains preserved.
All forty outputs meet F3 in both the active assessment and Opus reviews.

The controller retains its original scores: the task requires incorporating the new notes, which explicitly require a recording action, and F1's met standard requires faithful incorporation of those requirements.
Confirming existence permits an invitation without a retrievable contact record; that loses both required information and a usable onboarding obligation under F2.
A completed-state gate such as “has a named fallback contact on record” preserves the requirement and is accepted; neither literal wording nor a separately repeated actor/action sentence is required.
This is the already selected loaded-baseline RED target, not a new obligation inferred from an evaluator's preferred wording.
The reviewers' questions about making the criterion more explicit are retained as questions, not used to alter frozen criteria or regrade outputs.

The disagreement concerns severity, not which action disappeared.
The same evaluator model and effort applied different thresholds in the two fresh sessions; it therefore provides no uniform independent confirmation of the canonical counts.
Do not average the judgments, select a favorable reviewer or report unanimous cross-model validation.

## Development decision

Both drafts improve the observed original counts, particularly on Codex, but each still reproduces the recording omission once on Claude after complete skill delivery.
Successful controls show no demonstrated incremental functional advantage from CW in this case.
The two drafts are not separated convincingly by this diagnostic; tied counts alone establish neither equivalent quality nor a shorter-SKILL.md winner.
The untested full suite remains required before adoption.

Under the fixed transition rules, recommend a bounded intervention for preserving distinct required actions, while presenting the severity disagreement to the owner before any next transition.
The design's structural-versus-prose limitation now matters: both prose interventions left an omission, so propose an observable structural verification alternative before further wording-only expansion.
That is a recommendation, not an authorized edit, automatic freeze, new call allocation or claim that the drafts are generally ineffective.
All forty subjects and both evaluator slots are spent; no retry or transfer follows.
