### Disputed control sequence

Codex document-addition control repetition 5 is the only explicit-body factor disagreement.
The source says: “The affected team can resume only after Omar has investigated and Nia approves resumption.”
The output says: “Omar must investigate the report, and the affected team may resume the preview only after Nia approves resumption.”
The complete [saved output](/Users/simon/work/personal/skill-study-private/concise-writing/revised-body-discovery-20261018/review-inputs/codex-document-addition/outputs/control-5.md) retains the precautionary pause, restricted evidence, other-team boundary and separate later expansion decision.
The active assessor reads investigation and approval as the required sequence with a precision weakness; Opus reads investigation as a separate obligation and fails F1 because approval could permit earlier resumption.
Reading the complete output does not explicitly restore investigation-before-resumption elsewhere, but the conjunction/context can carry that ordering for a reader.
The reconciliation therefore records **insufficient evidence to settle that consequential interpretation**, preserving the original active pass and Opus not-met judgment rather than selecting the stricter reading by default.
The [manual transcription](judgments.json) retains both factor sets and the independent narrative; no canonical subject result or frozen prior was overwritten.
This unresolved control observation does not establish a loss caused by applying either CW body or justify another body edit.

## Limits and transition

Five repetitions per cell, visible condition labels, exposed development cases and residual order imbalance prevent reliability or equivalence claims.
The models expose no immutable backend revision; earlier CLI strata and historical assessments are not pooled into these counts.
Some reviews mention unavailable linked historical records; the packets supplied the complete operative task/source, current policy and prospective interpretation, and no missing operative rule was identified as changing an outcome.
N3 reviewers did not receive the neutral setup wrapper: its required unchanged baseline commit was initially questioned by the Claude reviewer, then explained by the prior assessment.
The controller verified that authorization in the frozen native prompt and trace; it is setup, not an unauthorized task side effect, and no artifact/reply judgment changed.
Document completion replies are outside the mounted F1-F3 artifact scope; N3 is different and includes the entire assistant stream for its literal-reply observation.
Reviewer narratives occasionally say “same-session” writing/assessment; subjects and reviews actually ran in separate fresh contexts, while the active controller had authoring exposure.
These disclosures qualify the review rather than rewriting it.

All comment files pass parse/executable-token preservation checks; no subject-produced code was executed by the controller.
The hook suite passes 263 tests with 3 skips, and all four focused subject assessments pass structural/reference validation.
The broader runner suite is not claimed green: six host-dependent offline dummy-provider process smokes remain an open [plan follow-up](../../../../../plans/2026-09-11-model-driven-skill-testing.md), documented in the inspection amendment.
Actual pinned subject/reviewer runtimes passed the retained zero-model qualification; the unrelated offline issue is not a real Claude authentication failure.

The complete supplied original SKILL.md is 860 words; revised targeted-examples is 971, measured with wc -w including frontmatter/Markdown.
No overall consequential-quality advantage for the revised body is established across these focused cases; the local Claude comment preference remains explicit.
Matching pass counts do not prove equivalence, and output length does not decide adoption.
The owner prefers the shorter SKILL.md where consequential effectiveness and quality are equivalent.

No confirmed revised-body regression blocks eligibility for remaining coverage under the frozen rule.
Eligibility is not approval: the separate [forecast](../../../drafts/baseline-iteration/discovery/proposal.md#focused-allocation-and-later-forecast) is 54 body-regression subjects/six reviews for A, B and explicit N2, plus 36 discovery-regression subjects/four reviews for N1/N2.
Neither new description meets the observed cross-provider advancement rule; selecting another description approach or accepting that trade-off is an owner decision before further discovery testing.
Changing wording would require a concrete affected-revalidation proposal rather than treating old observations as validation of new bytes.
Further edits, compression, the forecast 90 subjects/ten reviews and live adoption remain unapproved.
The next owner discussion should weigh the local revised-body benefit against its extra instructions, resolve the discovery trade-off, and select only the justified remaining scope.
