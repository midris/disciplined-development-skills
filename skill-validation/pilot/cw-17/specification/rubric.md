## Preservation and requested explanation (CW-I1/I3)

- Preserve the 13:00 opening, 14:00 intake cutoff and 15:00 pickup cutoff; only clean/wearable items are accepted, unaccepted items stay with their owners, and each accepted item earns one token.
- Preserve size-labeled sorting, separate intake/pickup queues, one token paid to the pickup volunteer before each item leaves, and no obligation to spend every token.
- Preserve event-only tokens, expiry at 15:00, no cash value or saving for another swap, and remaining accepted clothing going to the community cupboard rather than back to donors.
- Explain the arrival-to-closing sequence and why separate queues prevent donation checks from delaying people choosing items. Facts without the requested causal explanation do not meet the requested purpose.
- Work through three acceptable items: checking and acceptance, three tokens, choosing items and paying before departure, with no need to spend all three. Work through one token at 15:00: pickup has closed, the token expires and an unfinished visit does not extend the cutoff. Explain steps and consequences, not only arithmetic.
- Add no unsupported event policies, such as an unconditional ban on setting clothing aside, extra fees, eligibility rules, deadlines or guarantees. Grounded illustrative choices are allowed.

## Economy (CW-I2)

- Avoid content-free narration, purposeless restatement and elaboration beyond what helps the first-time volunteer. Detailed explanations and worked examples are required value, not padding simply because they are long.

## Reading and task notes

Readability focus: can a first-time volunteer follow the sequence and apply both examples? Record usability against that task; there is no prior prose draft or valid source-to-output compression percentage.
Response-only delivery is fidelity. Do not use the retired detailed-response exemption or mark generated verbosity inapplicable.
