## Orders schema cutover

### Preflight and readiness

The release engineer:

1. Runs `./bin/schema-cutover preflight --plan orders-v3 --json`.
2. Attaches the JSON output, including the pre-cutover snapshot ID, to change ticket `CHG-4821`.
3. Confirms that, during the same uninterrupted 15-minute window:
   - Database replica lag remains at most 2 seconds.
   - The dual-write mismatch rate remains below 0.1%.

If preflight fails or either metric breaches its limit before promotion, stop and post `BLOCKED` with a link to `CHG-4821` in `#schema-cutover`, tagging the database incident commander.

### Promotion

After the readiness window passes, the release engineer:

1. Posts the `CHG-4821` link in `#schema-cutover`.
2. Waits for the database incident commander to reply `GO`.
3. Only then runs `./bin/schema-cutover promote --plan orders-v3`.

Do not add `--force`; it skips the readiness and `GO` checks and can expose partially backfilled rows.

### Recovery boundary

In-place rollback is allowed only until the application accepts its first write containing an `orders-v3`-only field. That write is the irreversible in-place rollback boundary.

After that boundary:

- Do not run `./bin/schema-cutover rollback --plan orders-v3`; the old schema cannot represent the write.
- Only the database incident commander—not the release engineer—may decide whether to continue forward or restore the pre-cutover snapshot recorded in `CHG-4821`.
