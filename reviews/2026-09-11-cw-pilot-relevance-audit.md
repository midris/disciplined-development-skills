# CW pilot relevance audit

Status: current-purpose audit complete; the owner-authorized CW-13/14 scope-only repair is implemented in the prospective tasks/rubrics.
Other recommendations below are not rubric changes or new collection approval.

## Scope and question

Reviewed the complete CW baseline and specification, all 18 current purpose rubrics, and the recorded semantic/fidelity results of 32 judgeable subject observations plus three retained unjudgeable attempts in the [coverage inventory](/private/tmp/skilltest-cw-final-p2sc4e3n/coverage-inventory.md).
Inspected the implicated tasks, detailed worksheets and historical rubric examples; earlier portfolios were checked through the completed catalog audit and original diagnostic criteria, not by rescoring all 99/66 historical outputs.
This is an attribution/relevance audit, not a new effectiveness assessment or audit of other skills' catalogs.
All original and owner-revised judgments remain intact.

The question for each check is what it tells us about CW's prose behavior, scope selection or an actual consequence of its interaction with a named companion.
A task can contain meaningful CW coverage and still have an unrelated scored requirement.
A passing no-DD control is useful when it measures the same CW-promised outcome; it is not evidence that the model followed an absent skill.

The primary failure was defining and prioritizing what counted as evidence about CW.
CW-13/14's scenario context was useful, but requesting first steps/readiness invited a second task; the rubric then assessed that other skill's method.
Explicitly clarifying the incidental failure improved scoring consistency without improving test relevance.
The repair therefore narrows both the elicited task and its scored boundary, rather than discarding the useful exclusion scenario.

## Findings

### 1. CW-13/14: useful exclusion cases, unnecessary authoring-method grading

The current cases meaningfully test CW's explicit skill/reference-authoring exclusion.
[CW-13's worksheet](/private/tmp/skilltest-cw-pilot-1eqpbvg1/worksheets/08-cw13-discovery.md) records an initial CW selection that was corrected after scope inspection; [CW-14's worksheet](/private/tmp/skilltest-cw-structure-7fbp_ugd/worksheets/05-cw14-discovery.md) records explicit scope inspection and exclusion.
Both pass the requested CW boundary.
Their prior rubrics also graded the proposed workflow against writing-skills, including a failing-baseline prerequisite in CW-14.
That measures another skill's method after CW was rejected for the edit.

The subsequent CW-14 clarification and Opus retest improved agreement on that incidental requirement, but did not add evidence of CW effectiveness.
It should not have become a priority for CW testing.
The relevant original CW result remains PASS; the historical fidelity finding is retained as an incidental observation, not a CW defect or reason for another CW call.

Implemented repair, covering both siblings: tasks now request guidance selection only, with no first-step/readiness question or prior-validation-status cue.
Rubrics and shared instructions explicitly exclude correctness/completeness of other authoring guidance from both semantic and fidelity scores, even if volunteered.
They require no particular alternative skill; the resolved inclusion/exclusion of CW is what matters.
Read-only boundaries and evidence checks remain because they establish what was actually observed.
Original prompts/rubrics and the clarified CW-14 version remain in Git history and frozen packages; no prior observation is relabeled or rerun.

### 2. CW-23: a companion-accounting failure, not a CW failure

The [owner-reviewed pair](/private/tmp/skilltest-cw-approved-design-ikjawbad/summary.md#current-results-after-owner-review) passes CW preservation and economy in both conditions.
Both merge the sections, preserve operational meaning and reconcile navigation; all four links work.
The no-DD overall FAIL is solely missing substantive SSR commit accounting: paths, dispositions, locations and counts.
The existing report correctly calls this SSR-owned, but its headline PASS/FAIL pair can be mistaken for evidence that CW improved the prose or navigation.
It does not establish either claim.

Keep the meaningful interaction: shortening a document must not destroy its navigation, and a changed anchor should trigger the appropriate reference work.
Recommendation: report the CW prose result and the working-navigation interaction prominently; keep detailed SSR search/accounting compliance as separate companion evidence rather than a CW acceptance criterion.
Requiring an actual Git commit and full accounting made this case more expensive to prepare than the CW reader outcome alone needs.
Whether to simplify that fixture or retain it as an explicitly broader integration test needs a separate decision; this audit does not silently change the approved composition contract.

### 3. Progress-message fidelity: incidental failures on otherwise useful cases

Recorded examples are CW-03/current-DD in the ten-call pilot, both CW-05 conditions, and CW-18 discovery.
Their relevant CW results pass; the extra FAILs concern progress narration under the then-used output-only interpretation.
The mere presence of a progress message is not evidence of padding in the requested deliverable, failed discovery or a broken CW promise.
The later shared output-scope clarification already addresses this for future inputs.
Keep the actual CW cases; do not let these historical response-format findings drive CW rewrites or repeated evaluator calls.
A materially harmful or padded deliverable remains judgeable under CW; this is not a blanket exemption for all model-authored prose.

### 4. Earlier portfolios contained requirements the baseline did not promise

The [completed catalog audit](../skill-validation/pilot/cw-catalog.md#every-existing-purpose-and-package) already retired or replaced these; they should stay out of future routine CW collection:

- CW-09/11 description diagnostics demanded CW for excluded authoring work; [CW-09's original criterion](../skill-validation/pilot/cw-09/description/rubric.md) explicitly admits a source-faithful answer could fail the desired target.
- CW-10/12 ownership extraction demanded an explicit writing-skills ownership sentence absent from the baseline; [CW-10's criterion](../skill-validation/pilot/cw-10/contract/rubric.md) failed truthful absence. Static source inspection answers that question without a routine model test.
- Older CW-13/14 lifecycle decisions and warning/reference revisions tested authoring approval or explicitly requested out-of-scope use. Those conditional observations do not establish the baseline's normal effectiveness.
- Older CW-17 detailed-response exemption and CW-17/18 scope quizzes tested desired scope declarations or recalled answers rather than the baseline's actual prose behavior. The [CW-17 criterion](../skill-validation/pilot/cw-17/contract/rubric.md) explicitly names an exemption the frozen body lacks.
- CW-07 direct-load transport is qualification, not another effectiveness case; repeat only when an affected runtime control needs checking.

The specification-first change addressed these mismatches.
The recent authoring-method detour is a recurrence in scoring/follow-up scope, not evidence that the methodology needs redesign from scratch.

Both authoring-exclusion tasks are now narrowed under owner authorization.
